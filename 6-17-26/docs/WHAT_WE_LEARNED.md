# What We Mainly Learned

Across the full investigation, diacritic and related orthographic perturbations are best understood as controlled input-representation interventions that alter tokenization and move internal activation/SAE neighborhoods; downstream generations sometimes enter different output regimes, especially under dense or visually unusual inputs, but the observed effects are mediated by tokenization, prompt register, model/provider behavior, and control-condition structure rather than by a stable model-independent "diacritic meaning" effect.

Technically, the strongest causal pattern is this: perturbing the orthographic surface form changes the model's effective input representation, and those representational changes can propagate into measurable residual/SAE displacement and altered generation modes, but current controls show that token-count inflation, visual novelty, ASCII corruption, semantic salience, branch prompts, and metaphysical/introspective register can produce overlapping effects.

The correct manuscript posture is therefore conservative. The project can claim that orthographic perturbations are a useful probe of representation and output-regime sensitivity. It should not claim that diacritics specifically induce introspection, selfhood, or a uniform stance change across models.
